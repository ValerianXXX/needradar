# NeedRadar: AI Strategy Case Study

## Executive recommendation

Use AI to prepare traceable opportunity evidence at scale, but keep source-rights exceptions, strategic priority, real-task interpretation, and investment decisions under accountable human ownership.

NeedRadar should be evaluated as a decision-quality system. Its first strategic question is not “How many ideas did the model generate?” It is “Did the organization make a better, faster, and more defensible decision about one workflow?”

## Context

Public discussions contain repeated descriptions of friction, workarounds, integration failures, and unmet tasks. They are useful discovery signals, but they are noisy, duplicated, multilingual, governed by different source policies, and easy to overinterpret.

A naive approach produces a trend feed. A useful strategy product must instead preserve the chain from source observation to bounded opportunity and from opportunity to validation decision.

## Decision problem

Three failure modes drive the design:

1. **Narrative inflation:** a plausible AI solution is described as proven demand.
2. **Evidence inflation:** many posts about the same event are treated as independent evidence.
3. **Automation inflation:** automated collection and drafting are treated as permission to publish or invest.

NeedRadar addresses these failures through explicit evidence levels, source-policy controls, authorship and source independence, semantic-overlap checks, and claim boundaries.

## Implemented operating model

The production pipeline performs six steps:

1. govern sources and consented submissions;
2. screen accessibility, noise, likely personal data, and rights flags;
3. structure user, task, trigger, workaround, desired outcome, and AI fit;
4. cluster compatible signals while retaining membership reasons;
5. draft counterevidence, unknowns, and human gates;
6. publish only when completeness and similarity gates pass.

The implementation produces public observations and opportunity briefs. It does not claim customer adoption or business value.

## Strategic design choices

### Evidence before narrative

Every brief must be understandable from its underlying observations. Source excerpts remain minimal and linked. The brief separates observed statements from editorial inference.

### Distinctness before volume

A lower number of defensible briefs is more useful than a large catalog of near-duplicates. Similarity gates compare the task, title, user, and evidence membership before publication.

### Accountable automation

AI performs reversible preparation: normalization, classification, clustering, drafting, comparison, and reason logging. People own decisions whose errors change rights, investment, or real-world outcomes.

### Validation as a ladder

NeedRadar distinguishes public observation, repeated signal, user confirmation, completed action, delivered prototype, real-task use, repeat use, and sustained outcome. A system may automate progression within a well-defined policy, but it must not rename a lower evidence level as a higher one.

## Production evidence

The 26 August 2026 public snapshot recorded 421 stored observations, 117 public observations, 10 published opportunity briefs, and 20 source policies. Twelve policies were active and five accepted submissions only.

This proves that the pipeline and public product operate. It does not prove that organizations will adopt the product, that users complete tasks faster, or that the product creates measurable financial value.

## Illustrative use-case decision

One published opportunity asks how specialist OCR workflows can preserve the source image when recognition is uncertain. The opportunity has a bounded input, visible uncertainty, a clear review handoff, and repeat evidence.

The correct strategic decision is not “scale.” It is **proceed to a 30-day discovery phase**:

- name one workflow owner;
- observe representative operators;
- establish time, error, and rework baselines;
- test a minimal routing flow for low-confidence regions;
- preserve pixels, coordinates, and correction history;
- decide whether to prototype, redesign, or stop.

## Transformation implications

The organizational change is small but important: product teams move from collecting ideas to maintaining a decision register. Each opportunity receives an owner, evidence state, risk statement, next validation action, and explicit stop condition.

This creates a reusable operating rhythm across strategy, product, engineering, legal, privacy, and domain experts.

## What I would measure next

For one bounded workflow, measure:

- time from raw signal to review-ready decision object;
- reviewer agreement on evidence level and publication decision;
- duplicate suppression precision;
- time, error, and rework in the target workflow;
- adoption and repeat-use behavior after a prototype is introduced;
- exceptions caused by source rights, privacy, or domain risk.

## Claim boundary

NeedRadar demonstrates strategy-to-production implementation. It does not yet demonstrate customer adoption, commercial value, ROI, willingness to pay, or product-market fit.
